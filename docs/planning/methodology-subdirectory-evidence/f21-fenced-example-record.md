# F21 — the tool's ledger entry goes above the first heading outside a fenced block (BL-101, plan section 7.6, S300)

Register row F21 ([`findings-register.md`](findings-register.md)). At the S300 Phase 0 picker he chose "F21 tool fix" as the deliverable (the recommended option, with two go-aheads: the owed `HANDOFFS.md` trim and the push of `ca2a653` to the fork, both done before the claim). The register named two shapes and left the choice open: skip fenced blocks, or insert after the `<!-- Entries go below -->` marker. This change takes the first, because the marker is optional text an adopter may have edited or deleted, and a fence is what made the example heading an example; it adds one refusal, on a malformed ledger, under the code the tool already has for it (`rewrite-unsafe`). Nothing was run in an adopter's real tree (the adopter work is in clones), nothing went upstream, and the tool is canonical-only, so no adopter receives it until D8.

## 1. The problem

`bin/migrate-layout`'s `insert_entry` put the tool's one ledger entry above the first line matching `^## YYYY-MM` or `^### YYYY-MM-DD · ` anywhere in the file. In `chat_verification`'s ledger (the v3.7 seed text) the first such line is the example `### 2026-01-15 · [ad hoc] Ship the export-retry fix` inside the code fence of the header's "How to add an entry" section, so in every apply arm the entry sat at line 48 of the moved ledger, inside that fence. No check saw it: `check-ledger` read 83 findings before and after ([`p16-chat-verification-record.md`](p16-chat-verification-record.md) section 5). It was fixed by hand there in `0a8ef90`.

## 2. The rule (the sibling readers were read first)

- `starter-kit/methodology_trim.py:516` `fence_scan` is the project's own fence reader and the one that decides where a ledger's records start: a line of three or more backticks or tildes at the start of a line opens a fence; only a line of the same character, at least as long and with nothing after it, closes it. The tool is standalone (it embeds no other tool's code), so the **rule** is copied and the **code** is not; the differential (section 4) is what keeps the copy honest.
- `bin/check-ledger:75-83` reads a simpler thing: any line whose left-stripped text starts with three backticks toggles a flag (no tildes, no length rule). It was left as it is.
- CommonMark (markdown-it, the oracle below) also reads fences indented one to three spaces, fences inside a quote or a list, and HTML blocks. The tool does not (section 5, item 1).

## 3. The change (`bin/migrate-layout`)

`first_heading(text)` (`:364`) returns `(offset, match)` of the first month heading or dated entry heading that is not inside a fenced block, or `None`, and raises `Unsafe` when a fence is still open at the end of the text with no heading outside a fence before it (everything after it is code, and any placement of the entry is inside the fence). `insert_entry` (`:388`) takes its position from it; the four branches (under the current month, a new month heading, above the first entry, at the end with the seed sentinel dropped) are unchanged. `HEADING_RE` and `FENCE_RE` are `:360` and `:361`. For a text with no fence line the answer is exactly the old one (512 of 512 in the random corpus of section 4, and every ledger version without a fence).

## 4. What was measured

- **Tests.** 16 new tests. **Ten were written before the code: 9 were red and 1 green by design** ([`f21-red-run-output.txt`](f21-red-run-output.txt); the green one is a fence that never closes *after* the first entry, which is not this tool's concern). **Six were written after it**: the command test for the refusal (the `rewrite-unsafe` path had no command-level test at all), four unit tests for behaviours my own mutant list named (a closing fence longer than the opener, a line of the other fence character, DOS line endings, a line of two backticks), and one after round 1 (an opener that carries an info string). The first ledger entry of this change, in `72a5921`, says "14 red before the code" of 15 tests: that was wrong, and this is the count. The whole file now reads `Ran 236 tests`, against the floor of 220 before.
- **The adopter it came from, in clones** ([`f21-adopter-check.sh`](f21-adopter-check.sh), [`f21-adopter-check-output.txt`](f21-adopter-check-output.txt)): `chat_verification` at `a3174b3` (the commit before its move), synced, then a real `--tier all --apply` by the tool as it stood before this change (`ca2a653`) and by the new one. **Before: the entry at line 48, inside a fence (`True`), the marker at line 185. After: the entry at line 183, the marker at line 181, inside a fence `False`.** `check-ledger` reads 83 findings in both moved ledgers and in the adopter's hand-corrected one: the defect was invisible to it, and so is the fix.
- **Against a real CommonMark parser** ([`f21-differential.py`](f21-differential.py), [`f21-differential-output.txt`](f21-differential-output.txt); markdown-it from `vscode_quarto_ext`'s `node_modules`). Part A: every distinct committed version of `CHANGELOG.md` or `methodology/CHANGELOG.md` in the 14 repositories under `~/Development` that hold a ledger (two more have none), 6,361 distinct versions, and the 6 distinct blobs of the seed (6,367 texts): **0 disagreements** with markdown-it on the first heading outside a block. Where the old rule landed inside a block: all 35 versions of `chat_verification`, **106 of `wsfct`'s 625 versions (none at its `HEAD`: an adopter not yet migrated, whose early ledger had the same header)**, and 1 of the 6 seed blobs. Part B: 20,000 seeded random ledger-shaped texts: 19,465 agree; 535 disagree and **every one holds an out-of-scope construct** (section 5, item 1); **0 disagree in scope**; the 512 texts with no fence line at all give the old rule's answer.
- **Mutation, two rounds** ([`f21-mutants.py`](f21-mutants.py), run by [`mutate-p7.py`](mutate-p7.py) in a scratch clone: `f21-mutation-round-1.txt`, `f21-mutation-round-2.txt`). Round 1, 22 mutants (what opens a fence, what closes one, the offset, the refusal, the four placements): 20 killed, 2 survived, **F06 and C03, one cause**: the nested-fence test closed its own fence by coincidence (its inner pair of three-backtick lines re-opened a fence over the heading). The fixture now holds an unbalanced inner line. Round 2 names the two again and four behaviours round 1 did not (the scan's start, trailing blanks on a closer, the returned offset, an opener that carries an info string): see the file for the result.

## 5. What this does not show

1. **Out of scope, by the trimmer's own reading:** a fence indented one to three spaces, a fence inside a block quote or a list, and a heading inside an HTML comment or block (markdown-it hides it; the tool, like the old rule, takes it for a heading), and a heading indented four spaces (code in CommonMark, not matched by the tool's `^###` anyway). The 535 random texts that disagree each hold at least one. **No real ledger version has one before its first heading**: part A's count of header regions holding an indented fence, a fence inside a quote or a list, a heading inside an HTML comment, a tilde fence or a fence of four backticks reads 0 for each, and its 0 disagreements say the same; that is the reason to leave them, not a proof about a ledger not yet written.
2. **The refusal is not measured on a real ledger:** none of the 6,367 texts has a fence that never closes before its first heading (part A prints 0). It is exercised by a unit test and by a command test (`rewrite-unsafe`, exit 1, the tree byte-identical).
3. **Only the ledgers on this machine.** An adopter whose header differs from the seeds (a hand-written one) is read by the rule, not by a measurement of it.
4. **`check-ledger`'s own fence reading** (any three backticks toggles; no tildes) disagrees with the tool's on a ledger that uses tildes or a longer outer fence. Not measured, not changed: it is the checker's, and no header region among the 6,367 holds a tilde fence or a fence of four backticks (part A counts both: 0).
5. **The hand-corrected ledger is not reproduced byte for byte:** the adopter's `0a8ef90` also carries its own explanatory entry and the 33-file count of the real move (the check ran with 32: the clone is synced from today's tool). The comparison that matters is the entry's place, which is the same (line 183, below the marker).
